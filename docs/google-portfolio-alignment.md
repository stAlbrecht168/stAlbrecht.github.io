## Role requirements → portfolio evidence

### Strategic UX leadership at scale

**What Google typically looks for:**
- Experience leading UX across large, complex product surfaces
- Ability to define and embed UX strategy across multiple teams
- Evidence of operating model design, not just design execution

**Portfolio evidence:**
- **Case Study 1: Scaling UX strategy across SAP Cloud ALM** — 7-step UX operating model across 6 agile scrum teams; design readiness scorecard; quality gates; design-led development process adopted product-wide
- **Homepage leadership section** — UX operating model pipeline diagram; role contribution matrix; design readiness framework; team capability building

---

### AI and emerging technology

**What Google typically looks for:**
- Applied experience designing for AI-first products
- Understanding of trust, transparency, and automation UX
- Ability to define AI UX quality criteria (not just visual design)

**Portfolio evidence:**
- **Case Study 2: Agentic UX principles into executable practice** — 6-dimension agent output evaluation framework; trust-first automation patterns; applied to SAP Joule
- **Homepage AI strategy section** — agent evaluation framework overview; Skills Registry AI practice strand

---

### Research as a strategic capability

**What Google typically looks for:**
- Research driving product direction and roadmap decisions, not just validating design
- Experience with formative research in complex or constrained domains
- Ability to synthesise research findings into strategic recommendations

**Portfolio evidence:**
- **Case Study 3: From field research to a new product direction** — contextual inquiry with field engineers; synthesis of physical and procedural constraints; findings contributed to strategic roadmap shift toward mobile/in-vehicle form factors
- **Homepage research section** — contextual inquiry; synthesis frameworks; evidence-based iteration

---

### Cross-functional influence

**What Google typically looks for:**
- Track record of aligning product, engineering, and business stakeholders around design direction
- Ability to translate design rationale into business and engineering language

**Portfolio evidence:**
- Operating model work (Case Study 1) — quality gates and design readiness scoring adopted by product managers and engineering leads across 6 teams
- Analytics-to-design synthesis (Earlier work section) — bridging usage data and design briefs across UX, product, and engineering
- BAUR research (Case Study 3) — research reframing a product strategy conversation, not just a design decision

---

## Cover note suggestions (for application use)

If a cover note is required alongside the portfolio:

- Open with the operating model work — it signals strategic UX leadership immediately, which is the primary filter at this role level
- Reference the BAUR case explicitly as evidence of research-as-strategy, not just research-as-validation
- For the AI case: emphasise the transition from principle to practice — the ability to operationalise strategic thinking is a distinguishing factor at lead level
- Close by noting availability and openness to further context on any project
